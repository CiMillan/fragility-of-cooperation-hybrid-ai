# The Fragility of Cooperation Under Hybrid Human–AI Societies

PhD research project, NOVA Information Management School (NOVA IMS), Universidade NOVA de Lisboa.

## Abstract

The rise of autonomous digital agents is reshaping cooperation in democratic and socio-technical systems, intensifying pressures from systemic shocks and polarization. This project asks when cooperation becomes fragile in hybrid human–AI societies, and whether mechanisms that support cooperation in human populations remain effective when human actors and AI-mediated agents interact under different adaptive logics.

The project treats cooperation as an evolutionary coordination problem in heterogeneous societies. Combining **Multi-Agent Systems (MAS)** and **Evolutionary Game Theory (EGT)**, it studies how local decision rules generate stable or unstable macro-level outcomes in social dilemmas relevant to Management and Information Systems, such as coordination, governance, reputation, and collective action. It also recognizes that AI agents are often proxies for human preferences or organizational decisions, rather than fully autonomous actors, which makes their role in hybrid systems especially important.

Methodologically, the project uses computational and data-scientific tools to model how different adaptive processes shape cooperation, including reinforcement-learning-based adaptation and human social learning. It further examines whether classical cooperation mechanisms, such as reputation and punishment, remain effective in hybrid settings.

## Research questions

1. How fragile or resilient are classical cooperation mechanisms in heterogeneous societies with different adaptive rules?
2. How does the interaction between machine-driven adaptation and human imitation affect cooperation?
3. Which structural interventions can sustain cooperation in hybrid human–AI societies?

## Objectives

1. **Systematic literature review** on hybrid cooperation across Management, Data Science, and Computational Social Science, identifying what is already known about cooperation in heterogeneous and networked systems and where the key theoretical and methodological gaps remain.
2. **Fragility and resilience of classical cooperation mechanisms** (reputation, punishment, norm enforcement) when applied to heterogeneous societies with different adaptive rules.
3. **Cognitive heterogeneity**: how different AI learning/decision processes interact with human social learning, and whether that coexistence strengthens cooperation, destabilizes it, or produces new equilibrium patterns.
4. **Structural interventions**: which interventions can sustain cooperation in hybrid human–AI societies, especially when external actors seek to shape the system through the strategic placement of LLM-based agents.

## Theoretical framework

The project models a finite population composed of two agent types, AI and human, each characterized by a strategy (Cooperate or Defect) updated through repeated interaction. A key premise: **AI agents and humans adapt differently**. AI agents adapt through reinforcement learning; human agents adapt through imitation (social learning). Strategy frequencies evolve based on relative fitness and the mix of adaptation rules present in the population.

The evolutionary outcome is sensitive to the underlying social dilemma (Prisoner's Dilemma, Public Goods Games, Common Pool Resource Dilemmas), the structure of interactions (networked vs. well-mixed populations), and the presence of social norms through reputation or punishment mechanisms. The project examines five classical mechanisms for the evolution of cooperation against this hybrid backdrop: network reciprocity, direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity, kin selection, and group selection.

## Research plan

**Task 1, Systematic Literature Review on Hybrid Cooperation.** Establishes the conceptual foundation through a review spanning Management, Data Science, and Computational Social Science, positioning the project at the intersection of these fields.

**Task 2, Fragility and Resilience of Cooperation Mechanisms.** Evaluates whether reputation, punishment, and norm enforcement remain effective once human and AI adaptive logics coexist in the same system, comparing which mechanisms hold, which weaken, and which break down under hybridization.

**Task 3, Cognitive Heterogeneity and Cooperation Dynamics.** Follows the experimental setup proposed by Pinheiro & Vasconcelos (2025), studying a population of adaptive agents across two-person (Prisoner's Dilemma) and N-person (Public Goods, Common Pool Resource) social dilemmas, to investigate how reinforcement learning and human imitation interact.

**Task 4, Strategic Interventions Under Imperfect Information.** Follows the experimental setup proposed by Pires & Santos (2025), embedding LLM-based agents with fixed strategies in an adaptive hybrid population, to examine which interventions sustain cooperation when the system is only partially observable.

## Interdisciplinary positioning

The project bridges Management and Data Science through MAS and EGT, treating cooperation as a dynamic coordination problem studied via mechanistic simulation and "what-if" scenarios: relevant to Management (governance, coordination, intervention) and to Data Science (how adaptive behavior emerges from complex data-generating systems). The expected contribution is a computational framework for understanding and governing cooperation in evolving socio-technical ecosystems.
